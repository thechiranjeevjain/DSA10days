## Executive judgement

The **operating plan is substantially built**: a dated September schedule, a selected cold-code wheel, a 263-link DSA ledger, a mixed daily October card, tests for its rotation, a mock workflow, and a scheduled job-link search. It addresses the central behavioral problem: preparation can end as a phase while interview practice continues.

**Interview readiness itself is not yet evidenced.** A scheduled cue is exposure, not an independently coded solution. The reported ~90 hands-on DSA attempts have not been matched to exact ledger rows; the new coach has no recorded practice events yet, which is expected because its start date is 5 October. The September scorecard, mock outcomes, application funnel, and one-day call response still need actual results. The wider LLD/HLD/Java/SQL/course material is not fully inventoried or mastered. Email delivery is not active.

**Recommended interpretation:** declare broad preparation closed on 30 September if the scheduled capability checks are attempted and scored, even when some are weak. Call the result **maintenance-ready**, then use cold mocks and actual interviews to establish or refute **interview-ready**. Do not equate either state with all 263 DSA solutions being instantly reproducible.

## 1. Reconstructed client brief: what success means

1. **End the infinite-prep phase this month.** Stop expanding the syllabus on 30 September 2026. Afterward, maintain capability inside a bounded schedule and switch the main job-change loop to applications, referrals, mocks, interviews, feedback, and offers.
2. **Stay callable without a rebuild.** On receiving an interview invitation, need at most about one day of role-specific refresh. Keep DSA, LLD, HLD, Java/backend, and relevant company stories retrievable, including variants and changed requirements.
3. **Use effort suited to a sprinter.** Work in focused blocks, consolidate, maintain, and recover. Avoid a rejection causing another 10-day loss. After three unproductive days, change mode. Preserve work, exercise, and relationships from the shared weekly/priority tables.
4. **Practice from blank and record proof.** The user has done roughly 90 DSA problems once, mostly from the seven-day DSA and company sheets. The broader repository has 263 distinct linked prompts in the current audit. Distinguish previous exposure, rapid recognition, runnable cold code, design explanation, and mock performance. Red/yellow failures should cause specific repair, not syllabus restart.
5. **Make the day executable.** The Markdown should resemble the older hourly WIN files: exact dates, times, links, problem order, stop times, S.M.A.R.T. checkpoints, and few morning decisions. In October, a circular mixed-domain system should choose the work automatically and adapt to evidence.
6. **Keep opportunities flowing.** Search India first (Delhi NCR and Bengaluru), then Dubai, Australia, Netherlands, Germany, wider Europe, and London. Use LinkedIn Jobs, Instahyre, Naukri, and employer sites; deliver fresh roles with official application links. Judge market conditions with current sources and the user's own application-to-screen funnel. The user also requested email delivery to the address supplied in chat.
7. **Use the existing materials intelligently.** The DSA codebase, 10–15 LLD problems, HLD projects, Java questions, company sheets, MocksPractice, résumé, Educative, and AlgoMonster should have explicit roles. Do not create another course-completion treadmill.

The user's **desired end state** has two parts that must be measured separately: (a) the preparation phase ends on time and remains sustainable; (b) interview performance is good enough under cold, timed conditions. An offer depends additionally on market fit and opportunity volume.

## 3. The binding trade-offs

| Tension | What the numbers imply | Audit decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **“All 263 cold on demand” vs a bounded life** | Six full DSA slots per build week and one recovery week in four give at most **18 distinct full attempts per four weeks** before repairs or repeats. One pass over 263 would take roughly **59 calendar weeks at theoretical capacity**. That still would not keep every solution cold. | Treat 263 as a diagnostic inventory. Test interview readiness on representative moves, variants, and mocks; keep a separate long-term full-library ambition if desired. Do not market the cue sweep as mastery. |
| **“2–3 hours daily” vs the shared weekly table** | The supplied weekly table protects office days, gym, sport, social life, and recovery. The current plan uses roughly 30 minutes Mon/Tue, two hours Wed–Fri, and up to three hours Sat/Sun. | Read 2–3 hours as an upper bound for deep-work days, not a compulsory daily minimum. If the user meant a strict daily minimum, the current plan does not meet that request. |
| **“No decisions left” vs actual interviews** | Prompt order and timeboxes can be fixed. Role fit, visa eligibility, which failure blocked performance, and whether a cue is truly green require judgement. | Automate scheduling and evidence capture; keep explicit human decisions at the few points where facts matter. |
| **“One-day refresh” vs unlearned topics** | A `U` cue is first acquisition, not forgotten material. A single day cannot reliably rebuild an unlearned design or coding pattern. | Make one-day readiness an **evidence gate**: repeated cold, role-matched mock performance plus no high-impact unknown for the booked format. The calendar by itself is not proof. |
